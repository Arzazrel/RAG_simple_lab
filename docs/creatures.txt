"Mirelark" is a small nocturnal bird that navigates using the faint glow of lichen rather than the stars. It builds its nest exclusively inside hollow basalt columns near cold springs. The Mirelark is known for a call that rises in pitch only when rain is approaching.

"Brindlewhal" is a freshwater mammal that stores oxygen in a muscular sac along its spine, letting it stay submerged for nearly two hours. It feeds by filtering clay from riverbeds and extracting microscopic worms. A mature Brindlewhal can weigh as much as a small car.

"Sythe Moth" is an insect whose wings are coated in a powder that hardens into a thin shell when touched by moonlight. During the day the wings stay soft and flexible. Collectors prize the Sythe Moth because each shell forms a unique fingerprint-like pattern.

"Ganuph" is a burrowing reptile that regulates its body temperature by swallowing warm pebbles at dawn and cold pebbles at dusk. It never drinks water directly, obtaining all its moisture from the pebbles it collects. The Ganuph is the only known animal to digest minerals for hydration.

"Pellow Crab" is a crab that changes the color of its shell to match the sound around it rather than the light. Loud environments turn its shell deep red, while silence turns it pale blue. Researchers use the Pellow Crab as a living noise meter in tidal pools.

"Vossen Fox" is a desert fox whose oversized ears collect dew at night, channeling droplets down to its mouth. It sleeps standing up to keep its ears raised at all times. The Vossen Fox can survive an entire month without a conventional water source.
